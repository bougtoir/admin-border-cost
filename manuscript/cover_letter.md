# Cover letter — Applied Geography

Dear Editors,

We submit "The cost of inherited borders: quantifying the
administrative-boundary constraint in electoral-district spatial
optimization across Kyoto and Shiga, Japan."

Applied Geography has long asked how formal spatial units shape, and are
shaped by, the functional geographies they contain. This paper contributes a
measurement framework to that conversation: a paired-optimization estimand
that prices an inherited administrative border inside electoral-district
design, computed at two resolutions to bound MAUP effects, linked to a real
commuting-flow network, and benchmarked against 200 matched placebo borders.
The Kyoto–Shiga case — a prefectural line bisecting a functioning commuting
field — returns a counterintuitive but well-identified result: the
constraint's best-found cost is zero at small-area scale and bounded at
municipality scale, and the placebo benchmark points the same way — most matched alternative borders admit no feasible constrained incumbent or are costlier,
so the inherited line is unusually well-placed. The framework, rather than
the magnitude, is the contribution: border constraints are priced, not
assumed.

The study is strictly nonpartisan (no electoral outcome variables anywhere),
fully reproducible end-to-end from official Japanese open data (e-Stat
statistical GIS, Census Table 6-1, IPSS projections, e-Gov statute), with a
published provenance ledger, QC report, claim–evidence matrix, and code.

The manuscript is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all
authors approve its submission.
