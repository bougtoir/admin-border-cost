## Desk-rejection triggers observed across desk-rejected submissions
(and how this retarget addresses them)

- Scope mismatch → the manuscript is reframed from an OR/planning
  paper into a territoriality/boundary paper: the estimand is the
  *price of an inherited institutional boundary* inside a
  representation system, not a facility-planning exercise.
- No engagement with the journal's recent literature → explicit
  conversation with the journal's electoral-geometry cluster, esp.
  Duchin & Tenner (2024, Political Geography), and with the
  territory/boundary canon (Agnew 1994; Newman & Paasi 1998;
  Painter 2010; Elden 2013; Paasi 2009).
- Case study without conceptual contribution → the contribution is
  reframed as *measurability of border cost* and the
  divide/entanglement typology, not "a map for Kyoto".
- Wrong-journal cover letter → new letter argues fit in the journal's
  own vocabulary.
- Unfinished presentation → same package-QC regime as before
  (36/36 checks, PDF-level inspection, OMML math, TNR, captions).

## Positioning

- Frame: "institutionally divided, functionally entangled" — the
  manuscript now leads with the territoriality literature, not with
  districting optimization.
- The Japanese setting is presented as an *extreme case* (closest
  prefectural-government pair in Japan; normalized entanglement
  top-ranked among the focal prefecture's borders) — i.e., a case of
  extreme spatial proximity, not a representative sample.
- Claim boundary: no causal "functional divide" language; watershed
  strictly associative; Level A = FEASIBLE + cost interval,
  Level B = best-found with diff = 0.
