# Citation Ecology — Political Geography retarget

2026-10-02 (UTC). Every addition below was verified on Crossref
(title, journal, year, DOI). The purpose is (a) to root the paper in
the journal's own conversation and (b) to keep the previous
planning/OR scaffolding only where it still earns its place.

## Clusters Political Geography works in, mapped against this paper

1. **Territory / the territorial trap** — the state as a taken-for-
   granted container (Agnew 1994); territory as political technology
   (Elden 2013); "territory beyond the fixed" (Painter 2010);
   boundaries as socio-spatial institutions (Newman & Paasi 1998;
   Paasi 2009). → Our estimand is exactly the *price of a taken-for-
   granted container* inside a representation system.
2. **Electoral geography & districting** — founding quantitative
   electoral geography (Taylor & Johnston 1979); contemporary
   computational redistricting (Duchin & Tenner 2024, *Political
   Geography* — the bridge reference: this journal already publishes
   computational geometry of representation). Existing redistricting
   methods literature retained.
3. **Functional territories** — OECD functional urban areas defined by
   commuting (OECD 2012), which legitimizes using OD data as the
   functional layer that inherited boundaries mis-measure.
4. **Optimization as measurement instrument** — kept minimal: the
   solver is a measuring device, not the subject (Daskin lineage
   stays, one line).

## Novelty boundary (what this paper does and does not claim)

- Claims: a *measurement* of what an inherited administrative
  boundary costs inside electoral representation; a falsification
  discipline (placebo borders); a case chosen by pre-specified
  criteria and reported including unfavourable comparisons.
- Does NOT claim: first/novel language; a causal functional divide;
  global validity of the Kyoto–Shiga magnitude; that Shiga–Kyoto is
  the unique or maximal entanglement in Japan (it is not on raw
  volume — Osaka dominates; and Nara→Osaka exceeds Shiga→Kyoto on
  the origin-normalized share).
