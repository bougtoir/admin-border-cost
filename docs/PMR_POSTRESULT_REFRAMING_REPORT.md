# Post-result reframing report (2026-10-06)

## What changed and why
The completed healthcare/education analyses show near-zero mean boundary costs for all
access functions. The earlier framing ("the same boundary may be expensive for one
function and nearly free for another") is not supported as an empirical headline.

## Replacements applied
- "Kyoto and Shiga provide an extreme test" → "an unusually sharp test in terms of
  institutional proximity and cross-border functional entanglement".
- "for spatially dense local services, prefectural congruence is nearly free, in
  contrast to its design-sensitive cost in the electoral case" → "Across
  representation, healthcare access, and education access, the aggregate cost of the
  inherited boundary is small; what varies is where that cost appears and how
  concentrated it is."
- Hypothesis reframed: heterogeneity lies in the spatial distribution of cost, not
  its average magnitude.
- New framing: SELECTIVE TERRITORIAL PERMEABILITY — retained boundary + targeted
  cross-boundary access where marginal burdens concentrate — labelled a design
  implication, not a tested intervention.
- Methods labelled accurately: uncapacitated nearest-facility argmin (closed-form),
  geodesic distance; not service-system optimization.
- Null magnitudes retained and made the point: aggregate cheapness does not imply no
  public-management problem.
